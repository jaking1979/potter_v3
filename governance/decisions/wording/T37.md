## Decision & Reconciliation Record — T37 AGREED 2026-10-27

<b>Exact agreed wording:</b>

1. <b>Potter-owned product boundary.</b> Potter's policy, approvals, tasks/responsibilities, memory staging, receipts/traces, health/Inspect state and client contract are Potter-owned and remain usable/inspectable independently of the OpenClaw harness. OpenClaw is the initial reusable runtime substrate under T8, accessed through a pinned adapter with contract tests; its Control UI is Advanced/diagnostic, not the ordinary Potter product. The Potter client contract is versioned and harness-neutral so a future T8 winner can replace OpenClaw without rewriting Potter's durable product layer.
2. <b>Everyday surfaces.</b> Conversation may be reached through supported channels, but no single third-party/native client is mandatory. Potter Home is an installable PWA with Today, Work, Activity, Memory and More; Telegram remains the independent alert/recovery channel. Home PWA is the default owned client if S11/S12 pass; the OpenClaw app is optional/fallback. iMessage is a candidate conversation channel only if the exact agreed S13 passes; if it passes, Josh's S12 real-use rating chooses the preferred conversation channel. Consequential approvals are never granted by typed "yes" in iMessage or by voice.
3. <b>One approval authority.</b> The T11/T12 PolicyEngine record is the sole approval authority. Telegram, Home and any supported native card are renderers of the same bound, single-use approval; resolving it anywhere clears the others. Voice may request an approval but never grant one. Money/send-as-Josh/unlock-class actions require the ratified policy plus passkey step-up where specified.
4. <b>Credentials and payments.</b> Potter does not build its own secrets vault and never stores raw payment-card numbers in ordinary Potter state. The vault is selected by the V1 representative isolation/cost/operational-burden test across viable candidates rather than precommitted to 1Password. When payment capability is un-deferred, use a tokenized/one-time approval-gated rail if it passes the owning tests. Secrets and payment credentials never enter model-visible context; any mechanism that would need them there requires a rule-12 revision of T11/T12.
5. <b>Household model.</b> v3 ships Josh's Potter only, but records, secrets, backups, identity and grants are domain-ready from day one. Every mutually private person uses the same isolation primitive selected by the S3 threat model/negative-access suite: containers if they satisfy the boundary; VMs for all such domains if they do not. Christie may receive her own Potter later if she wants it, after Josh's domain passes the T22 gate and 30 days of production use; Molly/Edie remain deferred until the T7 trigger, provider/parental-policy review and isolation suite pass. Child differences are policy differences, not weaker/stronger virtualization by age. Josh as Proxmox root is explicitly disclosed as a technical super-admin; admin access is logged/visible. Shared household resources use explicit scoped, revocable grants with receipts.
6. <b>Voice and calls.</b> Voice remains in v3 scope and is built/tested in increasing consequence: voice notes; live Potter voice; Home Assistant/ambient voice; calls involving Josh; business calls last. Business calling remains in scope because Josh explicitly wants it. A call record carries initiator, direction, counterparty class, identity confidence, disclosure evidence, transcript/recording policy, bounded mandate, allowed data/spend (spend envelope defaults to \$0), identity-doubt behavior, escalations and outcome receipt. S6 cannot pass until a cited scenario-specific legal/policy memo settles the actual call types. Carrier/provider choice follows measured reliability/cost/integration evidence; Telnyx is the current leading candidate, not an irrevocable dependency.
7. <b>Deferred high-consequence surfaces stay deferred.</b> Browser/computer-use consequences, autonomous commerce and other T7-deferred classes do not become available merely because OpenClaw or another component supports them. They reopen only through the ratified T7/T36 triggers and then pass through PolicyEngine. Generated OpenClaw configuration is hardened and CI-checked: Dreaming off where it conflicts with T15, explicit plugin allowlist, narrowed session visibility, public session links off, appropriate sandbox/exec restrictions, egress controls, and disabled camera/location/HealthKit unless separately opened by a ratified decision.
8. <b>Build repository and collaboration.</b> <code>jaking1979/potter_v3</code> is the canonical implementation repository. It begins as one monorepo unless a component later needs a materially different license/release boundary. No implementation PR opens before T37 ratification. The first PR after ratification is governance/contracts/CI scaffolding only. Ordinary work uses one agent author and the other as reviewer; interfaces/contracts land before dependent implementation; merge queue serializes changes; P0/P1 disagreements that touch product decisions return to the Council rather than being resolved by code-review authority. Josh is not a routine merge bottleneck.
9. <b>Decision-aware merge gate.</b> Code review authority cannot alter product-decision authority. Canonical Notion ratification remains the authority, but Decision Log text alone is not approval evidence. A ratification-sync job on the Potter host computes the exact decision/manifest wording hash and may sign a ratification artifact only when either (a) Notion attribution proves Josh's own user account made the ratifying edit, or (b) Josh approves a T19 card cryptographically bound to that exact hash. The signed artifact contains the decision ID, wording hash and approval evidence; its private signing key lives in the approved vault, while CI receives only the public verification key. CI permits a manifest-changing merge only when that artifact verifies. GitHub-mobile approval is a temporary fallback until this path passes a negative test proving that agent/integration-written “ratified” text without qualifying Josh evidence cannot produce a mergeable manifest. Josh is not a permanent GitHub approver.
10. <b>CI, staging, release and rollback.</b> Required gates cover lint/types/unit/release tests, OpenClaw adapter contracts, migrations including N-1 compatibility where required, secret scanning, dependency/license policy, SBOM, accessibility and cross-review. <code>main</code> deploys only to side-effect-safe staging first. Stable releases are versioned and signed; a host-side updater verifies the release, drains/snapshots/checkpoints, applies, runs drift/smoke/release checks and automatically rolls back on failure. Brooklyn follows the T35 promotion/fencing rules and lags primary promotion where needed. Josh sees plain-language update/progress/success-or-rollback UX, not Git operations.
11. <b>Product UX and failure UX.</b> Normal Potter use is conversation-first, with Home exposing only state that benefits from durable visual inspection/action. Approvals and blocked work surface in Today/Work rather than being buried in an admin console. Artifacts remain linked durable outputs rather than a required top-level tab. Connections, autonomy controls, household, health/updates, devices/passkeys and Advanced live under More. Degraded states must say what is unavailable, what still works, state age/staleness and what Josh should do; T25 Inspect remains usable when the harness is down. WCAG 2.2 AA is the launch target for Home.

No config-file editing is required in the normal flow after the initial host install. Export/import and harness replacement remain first-class so Potter is not locked to OpenClaw, a client, vault, carrier or payment provider.

S1–S13 are the acceptance evidence, including approval-renderer consistency, Home independence, domain isolation plus add/remove-person drill, voice latency and approval boundary, business-call drill, first-run usability, update/rollback, capacity, Home efficacy, native-client independence, hybrid/PWA-vs-app UX and the exact agreed S13 iMessage test. Releases carry an SBOM. Steady-state Josh upkeep target is ≤30 minutes/month; exceeding it in two consecutive monthly reviews triggers Council review of the burden-producing subsystem.
